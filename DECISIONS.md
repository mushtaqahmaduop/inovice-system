- **D-30** A **delivery charge** collected for a third-party driver is stored **beside `grand_total`, never inside it** (`invoices.delivery_fee`, AED fils, migration 0015). - **D-31** A **draft invoice may be deleted outright** — by an **admin**, or by the **employee who created it**, and by nobody else (owner request, 2026-07-27). A draft has no invoice number, no VAT, no customer copy and no payment: it is scratch, and §4.1 always intended it to be removable (the `invoices_delete_draft` RLS policy and the `invoices_draft_only_delete` trigger already permitted it). The only obstacle was §4.2's append-only guard on `invoice_events` — every draft owns a `created` event whose FK is `ON DELETE NO ACTION`, so the delete always failed.
  - Migration 0016 opens **one narrow door**: `invoice_events` gets its own guard function that tolerates a DELETE *only* when the transaction-local flag `app.draft_purge` holds the exact invoice id being purged **and** that invoice is still a draft. UPDATE is still never permitted, and **`payments` keeps the original unconditional `enforce_append_only()`**. Only `delete_draft()` sets the flag (`is_local = true`, so it dies with the transaction); a JWT caller cannot exploit it because `authenticated` holds no DELETE grant on `invoice_events` and RLS gives it no DELETE policy — layers 1 and 2 stop a direct PostgREST call whatever the flag says.
  - The permission rule lives **inside `delete_draft()`** (SECURITY DEFINER), not only in the API route, so a direct PostgREST RPC obeys it too. A sealed or voided invoice is refused there as well: **corrections stay a void + a new document, never a deletion.**
  - Each deletion writes exactly one row to **`deleted_drafts`** — invoice id, customer, line count, who created the draft and when, who deleted it, when. Both actors are stored as **id AND name**, because a tombstone must stay readable after the employee's row is gone (the FKs are `ON DELETE SET NULL`). The table is itself append-only and readable by admin and staff; the dashboard's Recent Activity merges it with `invoice_events` so a deletion is visible rather than a silent gap. **No money figure is recorded** — a draft's totals are unsealed, and an approximate amount in an audit table invites being read as a real one.

- **D-32** **Payment is a step in the new-invoice flow, taken before issuing** — step ⑤ *Payment*, an explicit **Paying now / Paying later** choice (client request 2026-07-30: *"the payment page should be before issuing invoice, a client could pay later or at the time"*). It replaces the "Client is paying now" checkbox that sat under the Summary totals.
  - **Paying now is the default** (the counter case), with the amount prefilled to the customer total and tracking it until the operator types their own figure. **Paying later collapses the fields** and the invoice is sealed **unpaid** — a first-class outcome, not the absence of an action.
  - **Full and partial are both supported now, and the remainder is collectible later**, in as many instalments as needed, through the sealed invoice's Payments panel. That panel deliberately **stays where it is**: it is the correct home for money that arrives after the fact, for part payments and for reversals, and a pay-later invoice depends on it.
  - **A draft still cannot hold a payment.** The API refuses it (*"Drafts carry no payments — issue the invoice first"*) because `payment_status` is derived from `SUM(payments)` against a total that does not exist until the seal, and `delete_draft()` refuses a draft with payments. So step ⑤ captures an **intent**: `confirmIssue()` seals the invoice, then posts the payment. "Before issuing" therefore means *decided* before issuing, not *stored* before issuing — this was stated to the client rather than worked around.
  - **Overpayment is blocked at issue** (allowed only on the sealed invoice, where it demands an explicit confirm). At the counter an amount above the total is far more likely a typo than a genuine overpayment, and correcting one costs a permanent reversal row in an append-only ledger.
  - A `reference` field was added to the at-issue payment (receipt no. / txn id), reaching parity with the Payments panel — it was previously hard-coded to `null`.
  - **KNOWN LIMITATION (open):** the seal and the payment are still **two separate requests**. If the payment POST fails after the seal succeeds, the invoice is sealed unpaid and a persistent error toast tells the operator to record it on the invoice page — nothing is lost or double-charged, but "issued & paid" can half-happen. Now that *Paying now* is the **default**, this path is the common one rather than the exception, so the fix matters more than it did: a wrapper function that calls `issue_invoice()` and inserts the payment in ONE transaction (`issue_invoice()` itself stays untouched). **Not yet built — awaiting the owner's go-ahead, since it needs a migration.**
